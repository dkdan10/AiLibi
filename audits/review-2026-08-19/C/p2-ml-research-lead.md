# AiLibi — portfolio read as an ML / LLM-agents research lead

Reviewer persona: research lead who screens repos/candidates for eval rigor, falsifiability, honest scoping, and whether the multi-agent substrate is something a lab could actually use. Time budget I gave it: the README (5 min), `docs/reading-guide.md` (10 min), the three named audits (skim, 15 min), `training/README.md` + one report (10 min), `tasks/phase-18.md` header (3 min), `eval/` skim (5 min), plus ~15 min running things. Everything marked [VERIFIED] I ran or read at HEAD `b809b19c` on 2026-08-18; [JUDGMENT] is opinion.

---

## 1. What this project IS (two sentences)

A deterministic, replay-first Among-Us-style social-deduction environment (room graph, scripted tactical movers, an LLM meeting layer with typed claims/observations/citations and a contradiction detector) with an unusually complete evidence trail: 100 committed games, per-tick state hashes, and pre-registered evaluation of a four-phase ES-based "learned impostor" program that beat the scripted mover on wins and was — correctly, by its own pre-registered bar — never shipped as the default. It is simultaneously a documented experiment in running ~350 agent-authored PRs against written contracts, and that second story has grown a private audit dialect that now sits between the reader and the science.

## 3. Strongest three / weakest three, for this audience

**Strongest**
1. **The eval hygiene is real, and I could check it in minutes.** [VERIFIED] The emergence pre-registration (`audits/audit-phase-18-emergence-preregistration.md`, PR #298, 2026-07-19) is committed in git *before* the finalist measurement (`results-finalist-eval.jsonl`, PR #317, 2026-07-31) and the ruling (PR #318, 2026-08-01). `paired_stats.py` reproduces the McNemar table exactly (shipped champion 15/9, p=0.3075 n.s.; two arms clear Bonferroni). The samples' vent-sighting cross-tab reproduces exactly from `tournament-eval-report.json` with 20 lines of stdlib (165 meetings; 70 flagged → 68 impostor/2 innocent; 95 unflagged → 10/21). Same-seed comparators, Wilson intervals, "UNRESOLVABLE" as an outcome class, additive errata. This is above most published LLM-agent evals.
2. **The negative results are published in full and the project's own headline finding undercuts its own marketing.** Two NO-FLIP rulings with the losing evidence committed; the "87% of catches ride a vent" table; "General social deduction: NOT demonstrated" in the outsider guide; N1/N2 recorded as effects that are real (z=+3.37, +4.32) but "NOT-DEMONSTRATED under the registered discipline" because the ablation clause was unsatisfiable. [JUDGMENT] N1/N2 are actually the most interesting *research* result in the repo — a learned impostor discovering that witnessed kills are cheap because the conviction engine only convicts on vent proof, and that starving flag supply beats venting — i.e., specification-gaming of a social-deduction referee. It is under-sold as such.
3. **The substrate is a usable environment.** [VERIFIED] Deterministic replays with per-tick SHA-256 (run-twice byte-identical in <1s; `verify_samples.sh` 100/100 in 3s), an enforced observation firewall, and meeting records whose turns carry typed `claims` (accusation/alibi/corroboration with confidence and evidence ids), typed `observations` (`saw_vent`, `saw_player`, `whereabouts`, `found_body` with ticks and rooms), `reply_to` chains, contradiction flags with subject ids, and every LLM call logged. Seed 17's opening turns read as a coherent fabricated-alibi attack on a truthful witness. This is a better research artifact than most "LLM Werewolf" repos.

**Weakest**
1. **Legibility cliff between the numbers and the reader.** The README status paragraph, the phase-18 header, and the close audits are written in an internal case-law dialect ("ladder tip", "adopting record", "canary denominator", "the §1.3 bar", "the 15.18 convention", "two-owner gate", "graduated CREW-ONLY", "referee FAIL at exact floor equality"). The reading guide's own §5.1 admits: "the corpus is case law with no glossary." A glossary is a patch; the underlying issue is that no artifact tells the ML story in the standard research shape (problem → environment → method → results table → limitations → related work) in ≤ 2 pages. `training/README.md` is a tier map, not that document.
2. **Apparatus-to-result ratio, and the bar's construction.** [VERIFIED] The shipped "learned champion" is 19 hex-float weights: a linear utility scorer over 9 option one-hots + 9 scalars (`agents/tactical/learned/forward.py`), trained by ES. Around it: ~29k LOC in `training/` (post-prune), ~26k LOC of training tests, ~17k LOC in `experiments/`, five 500–1,250-line close audits, a referee with population-relative conversion floors, a ballot surrogate (decision arm retired as all-SKIP), a conviction model, a composed runner, alternating-freeze co-evolution with hall-of-fame + PFSP-lite. The project's own audit says (`audit-phase-19-input-claude.md` §6): "Roughly 20% of the apparatus … delivered ~90% of the decision value … most of the rest was ceremony", and that the flip bar — floors pinned to the scripted FSM's own economy, a conversion floor that rises as the impostor suppresses evidence — is "close to unpassable by construction". [JUDGMENT] A research lead reads NO-FLIP ×2 against that bar as a *design* outcome, not a discovery, and reads the ceremony around it (owner ratification-by-merge, locked decisions, ledgers L1–L10, F13, "the two-owner gate") as process theatre for a one-person project.
3. **Generality and independence are narrower than the prose implies.** Every reported gameplay/ML number is on one model (`Qwen/Qwen3.6-27B`, non-thinking, via one hosted provider), one prompt set, n=50 seeds; there is no cross-model table and no related-work positioning (Werewolf/Avalon/Among-Us LLM benchmarks are not mentioned anywhere I read). The "two independent external audits" are Claude and Codex, commissioned by the same owner against the same tree; the reading guide should say "two AI auditors" in §1, not defer it to §5.2. And per the triage (§2 row 6, [VERIFIED] `git ls-files training/reports/_finalist_eval_raw` is empty), the 449-game finalist raw slate behind the adoption ruling is not in the repo — the derived cells reproduce, the raw evidence does not.

## 6. Would I advance / take the meeting / star?

- **Star the repo:** yes. The substrate + replay corpus + honest scoping are worth having in a lab's bookmarks.
- **Take the meeting:** yes, mostly to talk about eval design — the person clearly understands pre-registration, Goodhart separation, paired tests, and "the gate is not the reward", which is rarer than model-training skill. I would come in wanting to interrogate why four phases were spent re-measuring against a bar the project's own audit calls unpassable, and whether they can tell the story in ten minutes without the dialect.
- **Advance as a research candidate:** conditional. Rigor: yes. Taste: mixed — strong on measurement, weak on knowing when to stop building measurement. Communication: currently the blocker.
- **The single change that would most raise it:** a ≤ 2-page `docs/ml-program.md` (or a rewrite of `training/README.md`'s top) in standard research shape — problem, environment (obs/actions/reward in one paragraph, one figure), method (ES over a 19-weight utility scorer; the referee as selection gate), results (one table: arm, win vs same-seed FSM, McNemar p, referee verdict), the two behavioral findings N1/N2 framed as referee-exploitation, limitations (one model, n=50, bar construction, raw slate off-repo), related work — with the README's status paragraph replaced by three sentences that link to it. Everything needed already exists across the guide §6, the input audit §6, and `training/README.md` §3; it needs one author and no new jargon.
