## 1. Bottom line

Both audits are unusually good — materially better than typical external review: nearly every checkable claim in both reproduced against the bytes, including three independent recomputations of the central gameplay cross-tab. One source-audit term does require correction: Claude calls the conviction model's `90/96 = 0.9375` result "decision accuracy," but the committed report defines it as **conversion-label accuracy**; composed meeting-decision accuracy is `83/96 = 0.8646`. The audits otherwise converge on the four findings that should define Phase 19: (1) the project's prose layer (README, DESIGN.md, `.env.example`, in-code docstrings) has drifted into outright falsehood while the mechanical invariants (determinism, firewall, replay reconstruction) survive adversarial verification; (2) the headline "deduction" number is dominated by engine-donated vent proof — **87% of correct 9p ejections ride an ejectee-specific `vent_sighting` flag; without one, accuracy is ~30–39%** — and the weak-flag doctrine demonstrably convicts innocents; (3) the ML program should be frozen, its residual value being evaluation machinery and honest negative results, not campaign infrastructure; (4) the review-and-refresh charter is correct, but "refresh" must mean truth-and-narrative correction, not cosmetics. The largest apparent disagreement — Claude reports the full gate **green**, Codex reports it **red** — resolves by platform: Linux passes and Codex's Darwin-arm64 host does not. That establishes that the code's "bit-stable across machines" ES promise is false; `random.Random.gauss()`/libm variation is the leading cause, but neither audit isolated it experimentally. The internal ledger's main failure mode remains its **outward blind spot** — its 14-item §7 hand-off contains nothing about documentation truth, the spectator experience, or the games as games, where both external audits found the highest-value work.
